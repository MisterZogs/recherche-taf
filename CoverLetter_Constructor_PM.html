Dear Hiring Team,

For ten years I sat between L'Oréal's HR business teams and the developers building for them, across forty countries running on one platform. My job was to make integrations land: work out what the business actually needed, write the specification, arbitrate what got built, then stay with the release through go-live and the support queue that followed. That is where I learned that the real cost of an integration surfaces months later, in the defects nobody instrumented for and the escalations that end up on a sponsor's desk.

Time-to-value gets decided long before go-live, in how clearly the setup path is written down and in how much of the diagnosis a customer can do before opening a ticket. On OneProfile I owned that path across forty countries; on OnePayroll I ran the data migration into production. Both jobs taught me to treat a broken data flow as something to design out of the product, since the support queue only ever shows you the symptom.

My product ownership comes from WallOfTraders.com, the SaaS platform I co-founded and ran as CEO, where I decided what shipped, specified it, and checked it against how users actually behaved. That is real product work at a smaller scale. Attribution and browser constraints would be new territory, and I would be learning those specifics on the job.
